Statistics in the admin: figures

Statistics in the admin: figures

The design of the admin’s “Statistics” area (/admin/statistics/{event}). The decisions (privacy rules, filter, table contract, CSV, chart library) are in ADR 0019; this note lists the data and the figures.

Goal

An organizer, during and after an event, answers: did the process work (proposals → votes → sessions → attendance), did the venue work (rooms, gaps, peak times), and which features people used (1-on-1s, profiles, comments, notifications). Every figure is a table that downloads as CSV, and most have a chart. The same page serves a past event after an upgrade.

What the data supports today

Data Has time? Note
proposals created_time proposal timeline, “late proposals get fewer votes”
votes no no voting timeline
sessions start/end; no creation time origin derived (ADR 0019)
rsvps no no RSVP timeline, no cancellations
attendee count — host-reported, nullable (NULL = not reported)
comments, likes created_time profile comments have no event: scope by profile’s membership
meetings created_at, responded_at responded_at = last status change; expired derived
meeting availability slot who offered how much
notifications created_at, read_at read rate per type; guests can delete them
email settings, push no per guest, global: report over the event’s members
change log yes, but pruned not a source for history
login sessions yes, but expire after 30 days not a source for history

Past events. An organizer upgrades an old database (e.g. 3.8.1) and opens the page. A figure whose data an older release did not collect (attendee counts before 3.6.0, 1-on-1s before they existed) says “No data”, never 0. The release-upgrade fixtures check that the page works on every migrated release.

Figures

Each section is a set of figures. A figure is: a table (sortable, CSV) and, where it helps, a chart. The SPA shows each as a card with an explanation (features/admin/stats-figures.tsx, the catalogue); a big table’s charts get cards of their own there. Bold marks the figures we think matter most.

1. Overview

  • Headline numbers: members, active members (voted, RSVPed, proposed, hosted, commented or offered 1-on-1 times), voters, RSVPers, proposals, sessions, hosts, sessions with an attendee report.
  • Participation funnel (chart: horizontal bars): members → active → voted → RSVPed → hosted.
  • Who did what (aggregate overlap): how many members voted only, RSVPed only, both, neither; mean RSVPs of voters vs non-voters. This answers “do voters engage more” without a joinable table.

2. Proposals and voting

  • Proposal funnel table, one row per proposal: title, hosts, created, duration, cohost wanted, interested / maybe / skip, voters, interest share s, rank, became a session, the session’s RSVPs, reported attendees, comments. This is the “votes → RSVPs → attendance” line asked for. Chart: ranked bars of interest, coloured by “became a session”.
  • Popular but never scheduled: the top-quartile proposals without a session. Short list, high value.
  • Late proposals get fewer votes? Scatter: created time vs voters, ρ. Tells the organizer whether the proposal phase or the voting phase was too short.
  • Proposals over time: proposals created per day per day (line; no phase bands).
  • Votes per voter (per-person, pseudonymous): total, interested, maybe, skip, share of proposals covered. Chart: histogram of totals. Also: share of voters who voted on every proposal.
  • Choice mix: interested / maybe / skip overall, and a histogram of each voter’s interested share (“picky vs generous” voters).
  • Hosts and proposals: proposals per host (histogram), co-hosted proposals, cohost wanted vs cohost found.
  • Conversion: proposals → sessions, by interest quartile. A proposal “became a session” if it has any session, even one the filter removes; its RSVPs and report come from a kept session only.

3. Sessions

  • Session table, one row per session: title, origin, day, start, end, duration, locations, hosts, capacity, RSVPs, fill (RSVPs / capacity), reported attendees, attendees per RSVP, closed, parallel sessions (others overlapping it), proposal votes, comments.
  • Sessions by popularity: ranked bars of RSVPs, with the capacity as a tick and reported attendees as a second mark.
  • Origins: count and median RSVPs per origin (proposal / spontaneous / organizer).
  • Hosts: sessions hosted, unique hosts, share of members who hosted, sessions per host (histogram), sessions with more than one host.
  • Attendance reports — did hosts report? Ended sessions with hosts, sessions reported, share; the same by origin and by day; hosts asked vs hosts who reported at least once; share reported where the follow-up reminder went out (a followUp row in session_reminders with sent_at or notified_at) vs where it did not. The answer decides how much every attendance figure below can be trusted.
  • Attendance: attendees vs RSVPs scatter with ρ; attendees per RSVP distribution (the “no-show” ratio, which can exceed 1).
  • Predicted vs real attendance: for sessions from proposals, the attendance model’s midpoint and 50% and 80% ranges (docs/dev/attendance-model/) next to the reported count. Inputs per event: N = members after the filter, P = mean parallel sessions over the booked time, s̄ = mean interest share. Figures: predicted vs real scatter with the ranges, and calibration (share of reports inside the 50% and 80% ranges; the model expects about 50% and 80%). Labelled “model fitted on one event, n = 13”. Each new event’s result is a test of the model.
  • Votes → RSVPs: scatter of interest share vs RSVPs for sessions from proposals, ρ.
  • Duration: histogram; median RSVPs and attendees per duration bucket; duration by origin.
  • Time and competition: median RSVPs by start hour and by day; RSVPs vs parallel sessions, ρ. Answers “were late-night or crowded slots weaker”.
  • Closed sessions: count, and their RSVPs and attendance vs the others.
  • Capacity: sessions where RSVPs exceeded capacity, by location.

4. Attendees (RSVP behaviour)

  • RSVPs per attendee (per-person, pseudonymous): RSVPs, days with RSVPs, hours booked, back-to-back pairs (gap ≤ the event’s break), overlapping pairs (double bookings), median gap. Chart: histogram of RSVPs.
  • Gaps between RSVPed sessions: histogram of gap lengths on the same day (0, ≤ break, ≤ 30 min, ≤ 1 h, ≤ 2 h, more). Answers “back to back or with breaks”.
  • Double bookings: share of attendees with overlapping RSVPs. A sign of hedging, and that RSVPs overstate attendance.
  • Hosts attend too: share of hosts with RSVPs to other sessions.

RSVPs are intent, not attendance; the section says so once.

5. Locations and schedule

  • Location table: sessions, booked hours, available hours (the days’ booking windows minus location unavailability), utilization, gaps, total gap time, longest gap, median RSVPs, capacity, sessions over capacity, bookable.
  • Gaps: a gap is free time inside the booking window. A gap that holds no whole grid slot (a session booked from slot S starts at S + break, so S must be at or after the gap start minus the break) is counted apart as an “unusable fragment” — they show where the grid wastes time.
  • Utilization heatmap: location × hour, per day.
  • Supply and demand by hour: parallel sessions per hour vs RSVPs per hour (two lines). The input for next year’s grid: where were there too many sessions for too few people.
  • Excluded sessions in a kept location count as occupied, not as sessions, so a room’s gaps stay true.

6. 1-on-1s

Only when the event has 1-on-1s enabled, or has availability or requests from when it did.

  • Adoption: members with availability, share; availability slots per person (histogram).
  • Requests: by status (pending, accepted, declined, canceled, expired), distinct requesters, distinct recipients, people with at least one accepted meeting.
  • Per person (pseudonymous): requests sent, received, accepted.
  • Response time: median of responded_at − created_at, for accepted and declined requests only. Every status change overwrites responded_at, so a cancel hides the response time; the page says so.
  • When: accepted meetings by hour and day.
  • Meeting points: the organizer’s points by use; free-text points only as “Other”.

A meeting with an excluded host on either side leaves every 1-on-1 figure. A pending request whose slot has started counts as expired. Availability outside the current days is not counted. Without availability, requests or the switch, the section has no tables.

7. Profiles

Over the event’s members: share with photo, about me, pronouns, based in, languages, prompts, contacts; profile fields filled per person (histogram); top languages and based-in values (k ≥ 3); contact types used (counts by type, never values). Languages and based-in values show only when at least 3 members share them; the rest is “Other”. Section absent when nobody filled in a profile.

8. Comments

Per target (proposal, session, profile): comments, distinct authors, replies, likes, edited, deleted. Comments per day. Comments per author (histogram). The most discussed proposals and sessions are a column in their tables, not a separate list. Comments by an excluded host and comments on filtered sessions or proposals leave; a deleted comment keeps no author, so only its target filters it. Deleted counts only tombstones: a deleted comment without replies is removed from the database. Profile comments have no event, so on a multi-event site they count in every event the member belongs to. Section absent when the event has no comments.

9. Notifications

  • Email settings: per setting, share of members with it on; members without email.
  • Push: members with at least one push subscription.
  • In-app: notifications sent per type and share read. Tells which notifications people act on. Notifications carry no event, so on a multi-event site this counts all events of the members, and a guest can delete one unread: the read share is a lower bound. Only members of the event count. Not built: a count of members who switched notifications off after receiving them (not stored).
